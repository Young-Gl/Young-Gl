我的第一份工作,是一个大学同学的朋友介绍的。我们只见过两次:一次在饭局,一次在电梯里。

Granovetter 1973 年研究波士顿的求职者,发现真正带来工作机会的,往往不是天天见面的密友,而是那些一年说不上几句话的熟人。密友和你的信息高度重合,点头之交才连着另一个圈子。

这个结论后来被反复验证,也被反复误用。它不是说人脉越广越好,而是说:维护少数几段深关系,和扩大一圈浅关系,回报的结构完全不一样。

我到现在也不知道,那位朋友的朋友为什么还记得我。

---

**Weak Ties**

My first job came from a friend of a college classmate. We had met twice: once at dinner, once in a lift.

In 1973 Granovetter studied job-seekers in Boston and found that the leads that mattered usually came from acquaintances — people you speak to a few times a year — not from close friends. Close friends share your information; acquaintances connect you to a different circle.

The finding has been replicated many times and misread just as often. It doesn't say that wider networks are better. It says that maintaining a few deep ties and widening a ring of shallow ones pay off in completely different ways.

I still don't know why that friend of a friend remembered me.
